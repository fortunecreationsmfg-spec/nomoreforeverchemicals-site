## What still matters from the 2019 floss study?

The paper is Boronow and colleagues, published in the *Journal of Exposure Science & Environmental Epidemiology* in 2019. In 178 women, reporting Oral-B Glide use was associated with 24.9% higher blood PFHxS. The 95% confidence interval ran from 0.2% to 55.7%. The same model did not find a significant Glide association for the other PFAS the paper reported. A fluorine screen was positive for all three Glide products tested and for three other flosses. Tom's of Maine Naturally Waxed Antiplaque Flat Floss was one of the 12 products with no detectable fluorine.

The American Dental Association said on January 14, 2019 that the data were not sufficient to support the headlines, and it still says to clean between your teeth every day. Consumer Reports, in the same article that explains how to avoid PFAS in floss, quotes dental experts that any floss is better than none. The full limits of the study are in the [dental floss explainer](/post/does-dental-floss-contain-pfas-pfas-free-floss-swaps).

## What did Oral-B say changed in 2026?

On March 31, 2026, Silent Spring Institute published "Oral-B stops using Teflon in its popular Glide dental floss." The article says Oral-B reports that updated Glide uses a micro-textured fiber, and that none of its floss products are made with PFAS. Co-author Laurel Schaider, quoted there, says other floss companies may still use PFAS, and that the practical step is to avoid floss with PTFE on the label and to look for uncoated floss or floss coated in wax, preferably from a natural source.

Oral-B's own page, [Dental Floss Types](https://oralb.com/en-us/oral-health/why-oral-b/floss/dental-floss-types-the-pros-and-cons/), checked for this guide on November 13, 2026, says the same thing in the company's words. "The new Glide floss fiber material is no longer formulated with PTFE. With the new micro-textured fiber, none of our floss products are formulated with PTFE or any type of PFAS." The page says the new fiber is "soft, gentle, nylon and an elastic material."

Two limits sit next to that sentence. It is the manufacturer's description of the current formula, not a new blood study and not a test this guide ran. A spool already in a drawer, or a store box that still lists PTFE, is the product Procter & Gamble described to Consumer Reports and the product the 2019 screen measured. Read the box in your hand.

## Which states restrict PFAS in dental floss?

Minnesota has prohibited the sale of dental floss with intentionally added PFAS since January 1, 2025, under Minnesota Statutes section 116.943. Colorado's Senate Bill 24-081 adds dental floss to the products that, on and after January 1, 2026, a person shall not sell, offer for sale, or distribute in the state if they contain intentionally added PFAS chemicals. Both laws are sales rules in those states. They are not a recall of a spool you already own. The Minnesota details, including how the state defines floss, are in the [explainer](/post/does-dental-floss-contain-pfas-pfas-free-floss-swaps).

## How do you read a floss listing?

Use the same checklist as the [explainer](/post/does-dental-floss-contain-pfas-pfas-free-floss-swaps), which follows Consumer Reports' January 2025 shopping list.

**Look for the words PFAS or PTFE.** "PFOA-free" rules out one chemical. A PTFE floss can be free of PFOA and still be a PFAS.

**Treat a missing fiber as a missing fact.** If the package does not name the fiber or the coating, Consumer Reports says to consider skipping it, especially when the marketing says the floss glides.

**Prefer a named wax.** Consumer Reports points to beeswax and to plant waxes: carnauba, candelilla, jojoba, rice bran, and castor. Uncoated floss is the other option CR names. Ask a dentist if you are not sure an uncoated floss suits your teeth.

**Keep the habit.** The ADA's advice after the 2019 study was to keep cleaning between your teeth every day. Switching the spool is the PFAS step. Skipping days is not.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is PTFE dental floss a PFAS?

Yes. PTFE, sold under the brand name Teflon, is a fluoropolymer. Silent Spring's FAQ on the 2019 study calls PTFE a PFAS polymer. The Minnesota Pollution Control Agency says PTFE is one member of the PFAS family.

### Did Oral-B remove PTFE from Glide?

Oral-B's floss page says the new Glide fiber is no longer formulated with PTFE, and that none of its floss products are formulated with PTFE or any PFAS. Silent Spring reported that account on March 31, 2026. The page says the new fiber is nylon and an elastic material. This guide did not test the new fiber. A box that still lists PTFE is the older formula.

### Does a brand saying "PFAS-free" mean this site tested the floss?

No. Each section above says who made the claim. For Cocofloss, Dr. Tung's, Boka, RiseWell, RADIUS, and Dental Lace, the no-PFAS or no-PTFE sentence this guide quotes is the brand's. For Tom's of Maine, the brand page checked here does not say it, and the sources are Consumer Reports plus the 2019 fluorine screen. This site did not run a laboratory test.

### Was Tom's of Maine floss fluorine-free in the 2019 screen?

The 2019 screen did not detect fluorine in Tom's of Maine Naturally Waxed Antiplaque Flat Floss. Fluorine is a marker. The test does not name which chemical was absent. A non-detect from products bought for that paper is not a certificate for a spool sold in 2026.

### Is silk floss vegan?

RADIUS says its silk floss is not vegan, because silk comes from silkworm cocoons. Dental Lace's silk FAQ describes cocoons imported from China and finished in Italy. RiseWell's floss is polyester with a vegan wax. Boka's coating is beeswax, which is not a vegan wax.

### Should you stop flossing until you switch products?

The ADA says to keep cleaning between your teeth every day and that the 2019 study was not cause to stop. Consumer Reports says any floss is better than none. If avoiding PTFE matters to you, replace the spool and keep the habit. Bleeding gums or a tight contact are reasons to ask a dentist which floss fits.

### Does "PFOA-free" on floss mean it is PFAS-free?

No. PFOA is one chemical. Procter & Gamble told Consumer Reports that the Glide material of that period did not contain PFOA or PFOS and that the material was PTFE. PTFE is itself a PFAS. Look for the words PFAS or PTFE.
