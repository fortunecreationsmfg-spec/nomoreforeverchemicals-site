## What Is PFAS and Why Does It Matter in Your Water?

PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) are a group of manmade chemicals used since the 1940s in everything from nonstick cookware to firefighting foam. They're called "forever chemicals" because their carbon-fluorine bond barely breaks down — meaning once they're in the water supply, they stay there for decades.

PFAS has been detected in roughly 45% of U.S. tap water samples, according to a U.S. Geological Survey study. In response, the EPA finalized its first-ever national drinking water standard for PFAS, setting strict limits for several compounds. Municipal water systems have until 2029 to come into compliance — which means your tap water right now may still exceed the new federal limits depending on where you live.

The takeaway: if you want PFAS out of your drinking water today, filtration is the most direct way to do it.

## Why Most Filters Don't Remove PFAS

This is the part that catches people off guard. Standard activated carbon pitcher filters — the kind most households already own — are not built to capture PFAS molecules. The pores in basic carbon filters are too large and the water moves through too quickly for meaningful PFAS reduction.

A few important facts to know before you buy anything:

- **Brita pitcher filters do not remove PFAS.** As of 2026, Brita has not released a PFAS-certified consumer pitcher, despite earlier announcements suggesting one was coming.
- **Boiling water does not help — it makes things worse.** Because PFAS compounds have a higher boiling point than water, boiling causes water to evaporate while the PFAS concentration in what's left actually increases.
- **Refrigerator filters are usually ineffective.** Most are basic carbon filters with the same limitations as a Brita pitcher, with a few certified exceptions.
- **Look for NSF/ANSI P473 certification specifically.** This is the only certification standard built around PFAS reduction. A filter can carry other NSF certifications (like NSF 53) without actually being tested for PFAS.

## The Technology That Actually Works

Independent research, including a widely cited Duke University and NC State study, found that reverse osmosis (RO) systems were consistently the most effective home filtration method, reducing PFAS levels by 94% or more in most tests. RO works by forcing water through a semipermeable membrane with pores small enough to physically block PFAS molecules — including shorter-chain variants that are notoriously harder to filter out.

Solid carbon block filters (different from loose carbon pitcher filters) are the next most effective option, using denser filtration media and longer water contact time to capture PFAS more reliably than a basic pitcher.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does boiling water remove PFAS?** No. Boiling concentrates PFAS rather than removing it, since the chemicals don't evaporate the way water does.

**Do refrigerator water filters remove PFAS?** Most do not. The vast majority use basic carbon filtration without PFAS-specific certification, though a small number of higher-end models have been independently tested with better results.

**Is reverse osmosis the only effective method?** It's the most consistently effective and widely tested method, but a small number of non-RO filters (carbon block and ion-exchange systems with P473 certification) have also demonstrated strong PFAS removal.

## Understanding PFAS and Its Impact

PFAS are not just a concern for water. They can be found in various products we use daily. From food packaging to cleaning supplies, these chemicals are pervasive. It's crucial to understand where PFAS might be hiding in your life.

### Everyday Products to Watch

Many household items may contain PFAS. Nonstick cookware, stain-resistant fabrics, and even some cosmetics can have these chemicals. Being aware of these products can help you make informed choices.

### Creating a Safer Home

To create a safer home, start by identifying products that may contain PFAS. Replace them with safer alternatives. Look for items labeled as PFAS-free. This simple step can significantly reduce your exposure.

### The Role of Filtration

Using a PFAS-free water filter is a vital step in ensuring your drinking water is safe. Filters that meet NSF/ANSI P473 certification are your best bet. They provide peace of mind, knowing that harmful chemicals are being removed.

## Conclusion

In summary, understanding PFAS is essential for protecting your health. Choose the right water filter, be mindful of everyday products, and take steps to create a safer home. Empower yourself with knowledge and make choices that benefit your family.
