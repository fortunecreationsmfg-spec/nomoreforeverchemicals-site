**Quick answer:** Some rain jackets contain PFAS. The chemical can sit in the durable water repellent finish that makes water bead, in a PTFE membrane that blocks rain, or in both. Toxic-Free Future's January 2022 tests found PFAS in 9 of 13 jackets and 15 of 20 outdoor apparel items sold as stain- or water-resistant. Two rain jackets in that set, from Mammut and The North Face, fell below the lab's fluorine screen. Gore's March 27, 2025 statement calls PTFE a member of the PFAS class and says its ePE membrane, launched for autumn/winter 2022, is expanded polyethylene made without intentionally added PFAS. A line that says only "PFOA-free" can describe a jacket that still uses another fluorinated finish. California and New York restrict sale of new apparel with intentionally added PFAS beginning in 2025. Both laws give outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions, gear for experts that is not sold for general use, until 2028. The Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry says dermal absorption of PFAS is limited and does not appear to be a significant route for the general population. The exposure these sources describe for a treated jacket is mostly the life of the product: making it, washing it, and throwing it away.

Below is what those tests, brand pages, and statutes actually say, how to read a hangtag, and which re-waterproofing bottles publish a PFAS-free line. Other household sources are in the [room-by-room guide](/post/your-room-by-room-guide-to-removing-forever-chemicals-from-your-home). PTFE on cookware is a separate guide, [PFAS-free pans](/post/best-pfas-free-cookware-nonstick-pan-alternatives-compared).

## Do rain jackets contain PFAS?

Some do. A jacket with no fluorinated chemistry also exists, and a 2022 test found examples of both in the same product category.

PFAS are a large class of fluorinated chemicals. California Health and Safety Code section 108970 and New York's apparel law both define the class as fluorinated organic chemicals containing at least one fully fluorinated carbon atom. EPA's page on health and environmental risks says there are thousands of PFAS, with different effects, and that most studies look at a smaller set of better-known compounds. A rain jacket does not get a single yes or no from the word "waterproof." The finish and the membrane are different layers, and a brand can change either one without changing the product name.

The record this guide can check is a 2022 product test, the words on current brand pages, and the sale rules in California, New York, and Washington. It is not a census of every shell on sale in 2026.

## What are a DWR finish and a waterproof membrane?

They do different jobs, and a jacket can have either one, both, or a version of each that is not fluorinated.

Toxic-Free Future's report, *Toxic Convenience*, describes the two uses this way. A surface treatment on the outside of the garment is often called a durable water repellent. A membrane is a layer laminated to the fabric. The report says a microporous membrane is built so rain droplets have trouble crossing from the outside while water vapor can still escape from the inside. Outerwear may contain a PFAS-based membrane, a PFAS surface treatment, or both. A tablecloth, by contrast, is the report's example of a product that is likely to have only a surface treatment.

Patagonia's care account of its own shells separates the same two layers. The membrane is what keeps water out. The DWR is what lets water bead and roll off the face fabric. Patagonia says the outside can look wet, which it calls wetting out, while the inside stays dry, because the membrane is still doing its job. Dirt, body oil, sunscreen, and smoke can all dull a DWR. The membrane, in that account, does not need the same refresh.

Nikwax's TX.Direct page makes the comfort point in the company's words. If the outer fabric absorbs water, Nikwax says the garment can lose up to 70 percent of its breathability, so the DWR has to be maintained. That 70 percent figure is Nikwax's statement about comfort. It is not a PFAS measurement.

REI's April 23, 2025 comparison of four REI Co-op rain jackets lists the same stack for each one: water-resistant fabric with a nonfluorinated DWR, a waterproof and breathable laminate, and taped seams. The article uses "nonfluorinated" for the DWR. It does not use that word for every laminate. One of the four, the Teris GTX, is described as a 2-layer GORE-TEX membrane. A nonfluorinated finish and a named membrane are two sentences. Read both.

## How is a PTFE membrane different from a side-chain fluorinated DWR?

One is a fluoropolymer film. The other is a surface treatment built from smaller fluorinated pieces that the treatment can later shed.

Toxic-Free Future says the PFAS surface treatments on textiles are side-chain fluorinated polymers. The building blocks it names are fluorotelomer alcohols. The report says those polymers break down over time and release smaller PFAS. In the jackets it sent for named-chemical testing, the lab looked for those alcohols and for persistent breakdown products such as PFOA and PFHxA. Total fluorine on a jacket was often much higher than the sum of the 51 named PFAS the lab could measure. The report's explanation is that much of the fluorine is in a polymer the named-chemical test does not count.

PTFE is that kind of polymer. Gore's March 27, 2025 statement says polytetrafluoroethylene is a member of the fluoropolymer class of PFAS and has been a component of many of its fabrics products. The same statement says Gore considers PTFE stable, non-toxic, not bioavailable, and not something that chemically degrades. That is the company's description of the polymer. It is still the company's own classification of PTFE as a PFAS. The 2022 report, looking at two Gore-Tex jackets bought then, read very high total fluorine, 4 to 8 percent, as the likely signature of an ePTFE membrane plus a PFAS DWR. ePTFE is expanded PTFE. Those jackets are not a test of a jacket built later on a different membrane.

The newer membrane has a different name. Gore's product page for next-generation GORE-TEX products says the ePE membrane is expanded polyethylene. The page calls the membrane PFAS-free and then defines the asterisk: made without intentionally added per- and polyfluorinated substances, and it may contain trace amounts. A separate line on that page says the PFAS-free laminate includes its DWR treatment, with the same asterisk. The March 27, 2025 statement says the brand launched the ePE membrane in autumn/winter 2022 and that by the end of 2025 the vast majority of its consumer fabrics products will be made with that membrane. "The vast majority" is the company's timeline for consumer fabrics. It is not a sentence that every Gore-Tex hangtag already means ePE. A jacket that says Gore-Tex, and does not say ePE, has not published this claim.

## Why doesn't "PFOA-free" mean "PFAS-free"?

PFOA is one chemical. The class is the rest of the fluorinated family, including a shorter-chain finish and a PTFE membrane.

Patagonia's account of its own finishes, on the story page updated August 2024, is the clearest brand timeline this guide found. In 2006 it started research on DWR finishes made without PFOA. In 2009 it began converting from long-chain C8 molecules, which it names as including PFOA and PFOS, to C6. In 2016 it finished that C8-to-C6 move across water-repellent finishes and membranes. In 2017, the page says, research showed C6 was also damaging. The page calls that a regrettable substitution and quotes the explanation that C6 still contains fluorocarbons. In 2019 it introduced the first gear with DWR finishes built without PFAS. In 2023 it says all H2No Performance Standard alpine rainwear, including a redesigned Torrentshell, was made without PFAS. For Spring 2025 and beyond, the page says all new styles are made without intentionally added PFAS, meaning the company does not add fluorinated chemistries to get a function in the gear. The same page says gear made without intentionally added PFAS is also made without intentionally added PFCs, PFOS, and PFOA. The order of those words matters. Dropping PFOA was an earlier step. Dropping the class was a later one.

NRDC's apparel scorecard from that period describes the same gap at other companies. It says some brands had pledged to eliminate only PFOA and PFOS, two PFAS already being phased out in the United States. It gives VF, the parent of The North Face and Timberland, as a company that had committed to phase out PFAS except PTFE. A PTFE exception is a fluoropolymer left in place. The scorecard is a 2022-era reading of public policies. It is not a 2026 audit of those brands.

Toxic-Free Future's later advice page says the same thing in shopping language. Avoid a label such as "PFOA-free" when the question is the whole class, because that phrase indicates the product is free of some PFAS and not all of them. Ask for a PFAS-free line, and ask which part of the jacket it covers.

## What did the 2022 outdoor-apparel tests find?

They found PFAS in most of the water-resistant outdoor apparel they bought, and they found water-resistant pieces with no fluorine above the screen.

The report is Toxic-Free Future, *Toxic Convenience: The hidden costs of forever chemicals in stain- and water-resistant products*, January 26, 2022. Staff bought 60 items in three categories from Amazon, Bed Bath & Beyond, Costco, Dick's Sporting Goods, Kohl's, Macy's, REI, Target, TJX, and Walmart, choosing mainly products advertised as stain- or water-resistant. An independent lab measured total fluorine. For most items the detection limit was 10 parts per million. Items above 100 ppm, the screening level, went to a second lab for 51 specific PFAS. Fluorine is a marker. The named-chemical test is a shorter list than the class.

Across the stain- and water-resistant items, 34 of 47, or 72 percent, contained PFAS. None of the 13 items without that marketing appeared to contain PFAS. The outdoor apparel subset was 20 items, all labeled stain- or water-resistant: 13 jackets, three shirts or pullovers, and four pairs of pants. The report says PFAS were detected in 15 of the 20. Eight of those 20 contained older PFAS, the long-chain compounds the report says were banned in the European Union and phased out by major U.S. manufacturers. Nine of 13 jackets were treated with PFAS. Five of those jackets contained only newer PFAS. Four, including three REI-brand jackets, contained a mix of older and newer compounds.

Two name-brand rain jackets, a Mammut Kento HS and The North Face Women's Resolve 2, tested below the fluorine screening level. The report says they appear to be made with alternatives to PFAS. A Patagonia Torrentshell in the same table contained newer PFAS and not the older compounds. That is a jacket purchased for a study published in January 2022. Patagonia's later timeline, above, puts a redesigned Torrentshell in the no-PFAS rainwear group in 2023 and puts all new styles there for Spring 2025. The test and the later company statement are about different production. This guide does not treat one as a retraction of the other.

The two Gore-Tex jackets in the REI brand group are the high-fluorine end of the jacket set. The report says total fluorine ran as high as 8 percent in the jackets it tested, and 4 to 8 percent in those Gore-Tex jackets. On that reading, plus the labels, the report says those two likely contained an ePTFE membrane and a PFAS DWR. It also says the lab found 6-, 8-, 10-, and 12-carbon fluorotelomer alcohols in them, and it notes a Gore claim that eight-carbon PFAS had been phased out in 2013. This guide did not find that 2013 sentence on the Gore pages it read in 2026. The measurement the report prints is the one to keep: those purchased jackets contained the eight-carbon alcohol along with the others.

NRDC's scorecard cites the same outdoor result in one line: in January 2022, Toxic-Free Future found PFAS in 15 of 20 outdoor apparel items. The scorecard is a policy review. The product test is the Toxic-Free Future report.

## Does wearing a rain jacket put PFAS into your body?

The health agencies do not describe skin contact with a jacket as the main route. They also do not publish a dose for the jacket in a closet. Those are different statements, and this guide does not fill the gap with a number.

EPA says CDC surveys show most people in the United States have been exposed to some PFAS. Most known exposures are relatively low, and some are high when a person is exposed to a concentrated source over a long time. Some PFAS can accumulate in the body. The routes EPA lists are work with the chemicals, drinking water, food including fish, swallowing contaminated soil or dust, breathing air that contains PFAS, and using products made with PFAS or packaged in materials that contain them. Household products and dust, on the same page, include stain- and water-repellent used on carpets, upholstery, clothing, and other fabrics. EPA says studies have shown that exposure to certain levels of certain PFAS may lead to reproductive effects, developmental effects in children, a higher risk of some cancers, a reduced immune response including a reduced vaccine response, interference with hormones, and higher cholesterol or a higher risk of obesity. The same page says research is still going on to determine how different levels of different PFAS lead to those effects, and that health effects are hard to specify because there are thousands of PFAS and the uses change.

ATSDR's clinician page, reviewed for this guide as updated July 7, 2026, is more specific about skin. Routes of exposure include ingestion, placental transfer, and inhalation. "Dermal absorption of PFAS is limited and does not appear to be a significant route of exposure for the general population." The ingestion section lists water-resistant clothing among products from which people may swallow residue or dust. It also says research has suggested that exposure to PFOA and PFOS from today's consumer products is generally lower than exposure from PFAS-contaminated drinking water. Children can take in more through hand-to-mouth contact with dust. None of that is a blood test of a person who wore a particular shell.

Patagonia's story page gives the company's version of the same split. It says the fluorinated finishes, when used in gear, are so strongly bonded to the fabric that wearing the gear does not present a health risk, and that the primary problem for apparel is manufacturing, when the chemicals can contaminate water or food, plus disposal, when they can affect soil and water around landfills and make recycling harder. That is a manufacturer's account. It matches the direction of the ATSDR sentence. It is not a measurement.

Toxic-Free Future describes the product life in more detail, citing other research rather than a new human study of its own. It says PFAS leave treated textiles in laundry water, that a full carbon-fluorine treatment can travel with fibers shed in the wash, and that disposal in a landfill is a long-term source because the surface treatments break down into persistent PFAS that can leach. Washington Ecology's regulatory determination says people can be exposed during manufacture, use, and disposal of PFAS-treated apparel, and that the chemicals can also be released to the environment at each of those stages. Use is on that list. The agencies above are why this guide does not translate "use" into a claim that the jacket is a significant skin dose. What is known is the presence of PFAS in some tested jackets, the dust and laundry and disposal pathways, and a limited dermal finding for the general population. What this guide does not have is a number for how much PFAS crosses the skin from one rain jacket.

## Which laws cover PFAS in rain jackets?

The rules below restrict sale of new goods in the named state. They are not a recall of a jacket already in a closet, and a statute in one state does not govern a store in another. "New, not previously used" is the phrase in the California and New York texts.

**California.** Assembly Bill 1817 added Health and Safety Code sections 108970 and 108971, Chapter 762 of 2022. The chapter took effect January 1, 2023. The sale rule starts later. Beginning January 1, 2025, no person shall manufacture, distribute, sell, or offer for sale in the state any new textile article that contains regulated PFAS. Apparel is a textile article, and apparel includes outdoor apparel.

Regulated PFAS means either of two things. The first is PFAS a manufacturer intentionally added for a functional or technical effect, including PFAS components of an added chemical and PFAS that are intentional breakdown products of an added chemical. The second is PFAS present at or above a total-organic-fluorine threshold: 100 parts per million beginning January 1, 2025, and 50 parts per million beginning January 1, 2027. A jacket can fail the rule on an intentional fluorinated DWR, or on a fluorine measurement, without the hangtag naming a chemical.

The exception is narrow. Paragraph (1) of the sale rule does not apply to outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions until January 1, 2028. Section 108970 defines that category as extreme and extended-use products designed for outdoor sports experts, for protection against extended exposure to extreme rain or extended immersion in water or wet conditions such as snow, to protect the user's health and safety, and not marketed for general consumer use. The examples in the statute are outerwear for offshore fishing, offshore sailing, whitewater kayaking, and mountaineering. A rain jacket marketed for hiking, commuting, or general use is outdoor apparel. It is in the 2025 rule. It is not the 2028 category.

From January 1, 2025 until that 2028 date, a new severe-wet piece that still contains regulated PFAS may be sold in the state only with a legible disclosure that says "Made with PFAS chemicals," including on an online listing. Manufacturers must use the least toxic alternative when they remove regulated PFAS, and they must give sellers a certificate of compliance. A distributor or retailer that is not the manufacturer is not in violation if it relied in good faith on that certificate. The shopper does not usually see the certificate. The public sentence, when the exception is still in use, is the "Made with PFAS chemicals" line.

One exclusion matters for the spray can, not the jacket. The definition of textile articles excludes treatments containing PFAS for use on converted textiles or leathers, because those treatments are regulated under the Safer Consumer Products program. The jacket rule and a bottle of aftermarket waterproofer are not the same rule.

**New York.** Environmental Conservation Law section 37-0121 uses a similar split. After January 1, 2025, no person shall sell or offer for sale any new apparel containing PFAS as intentionally added chemicals. The Department of Environmental Conservation's page on the law repeats that date and that trigger. Apparel includes outdoor apparel, defined as clothing intended primarily for outdoor activities, including hiking, camping, skiing, climbing, bicycling, and fishing. The apparel definition excludes professional uniforms and personal protective equipment, and it excludes outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions, which has its own date.

On or after January 1, 2028, the same ban reaches new outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions if it contains PFAS as intentionally added chemicals or at or above a level the department sets in regulation. The statutory definition matches California's: extreme and extended-use products for outdoor sports experts, not marketed for general consumer use, with the same examples of offshore fishing, offshore sailing, whitewater kayaking, and mountaineering. DEC's page adds a gloss, that the category is the sort of item that is "waterproof" rather than "water resistant." The statute's limit is the one that decides the category: expert use, not general consumer marketing. A general-use rain shell is in the 2025 apparel ban.

The statute also tells DEC to set a numeric level for apparel, effective one year after the regulation is adopted and no later than January 1, 2027. The DEC page reviewed for this guide still describes the rule in force as the intentionally added prohibition, and it describes an August 25, 2025 pre-rulemaking meeting. This guide does not treat a New York parts-per-million limit as adopted.

A seller is not in violation if it relied in good faith on the manufacturer's certificate of compliance. DEC says that certificate stays on site for inspection. It is not a label on the cuff.

**Washington.** The Department of Ecology adopted amendments to Chapter 173-337 WAC on November 20, 2025. The rule takes effect December 21, 2025. The new restrictions take effect January 1, 2027. Those restrictions cover intentionally added PFAS in apparel and accessories, and also in automotive washes and cleaning products. Apparel intended for extreme and extended use is not in the restriction list. It is in the reporting list, with footwear and gear for recreation and travel. Reports are due January 31, 2027, and then each January 31. The rule applies to manufacturers, distributors, and retailers of the covered products in Washington, including online sales.

Ecology's September 2024 regulatory determinations report is why the categories split that way. The department found safer, feasible, and available alternatives to PFAS for most types of apparel, including one PFAS-free durable water repellent. It did not find examples of alternative processes in use for extended-use products, and it found very limited evidence for shoes, so it did not find safer alternatives for gear, shoes, and extended-use products. The report's proposal, since adopted on that split, was a restriction for other apparel and a reporting requirement for the extended-use category. Rainwear is one of the examples Ecology gives for apparel. A consumer rain jacket is on the restriction side of that line. An expedition shell for extended extreme weather is the reporting side.

## How do you read a rain-jacket label?

Match the claim to the layer. A true sentence about the finish can sit on a jacket whose membrane is a different material.

**"Made with PFAS chemicals" is a legal disclosure, not a warning sticker a brand invented.** From 2025 through 2027, California requires that exact sentence on new severe-wet outdoor apparel that still contains regulated PFAS, including online. Seeing it means the seller is using the exception. Not seeing it does not prove a jacket in another state was tested.

**"Made without intentionally added PFAS" is the class claim, and it should name the parts.** Gore's ePE page applies it to the membrane and, separately, to the laminate including the DWR, and then says trace amounts may be present. Patagonia's Spring 2025 language applies it to new styles and defines it as not adding fluorinated chemistries for a function. An Amazon listing for one Torrentshell color, below, uses the sentence for fabrics, membrane, and finish. A sentence that names only the DWR does not describe the film underneath.

**"Nonfluorinated DWR" is a finish claim.** REI's 2025 comparison uses it for four Co-op jackets and still names GORE-TEX as the membrane on the Teris. Ask whether the membrane is ePE, polyurethane, or something the maker simply does not describe.

**"PFOA-free," "PFOS-free," and "PFC-free" are narrower phrases.** Patagonia's timeline is the reason. C6 chemistry was sold as the step away from PFOA and was still fluorinated. NRDC's scorecard flagged policies that stopped at PFOA and PFOS, or that carved PTFE out of a PFAS phaseout. Nikwax's product index describes TX.Direct Spray-On as "PFC and aerosol free." Its wash-in page uses the words "PFAS free." This guide does not treat those two Nikwax sentences as the same sentence.

**A Gore-Tex hangtag is a brand, not a chemistry, until it says ePE.** The 2022 jackets with Gore-Tex labels were the high-fluorine pair. The ePE page is the later claim, with the trace footnote.

**A 2022 non-detect is not a current certificate, and a current claim is not a fluorine test.** Mammut and The North Face were below 100 ppm fluorine in that study. This guide did not retest those models. The Patagonia Torrentshell in that study had newer PFAS. Patagonia's later pages say the rainwear moved. Read the date of the test and the date of the claim.

## How do you re-waterproof a jacket without adding PFAS?

Clean it first. Then add a treatment whose maker uses the words PFAS-free for that bottle. A heavy fluorinated spray is the product Toxic-Free Future tells people to leave in the cabinet.

Nikwax's TX.Direct Wash-In page says to start with Tech Wash. Dirt, and the film left by a conventional detergent, can make a jacket soak up water even when the DWR is still there. If the jacket is fairly new or was recently re-treated, Nikwax says cleaning alone can revive the finish. Once it has been worn hard, the page says to add DWR with TX.Direct, in a separate wash, after Tech Wash, and not to dry the jacket between those two cycles. The UK page calls the wash-in "non flammable and PFAS free." It says the treatment can air dry, or tumble on low if the care label allows, and that it is meant for breathable waterproof clothing, including Gore-Tex. The Amazon listing for ASIN B0019GOKK0 repeats a PFAS-free formula line and says household detergents leave a water-attracting residue, so Tech Wash comes first.

The Tech Wash listing this guide could confirm in stock is ASIN B000LN5O8O, the 10 fl oz bottle. A bullet says Nikwax products are PFAS-free, water-based, and non-persistent, and that the cleaner is for use before TX.Direct. The same spec table also prints a capacity of 1 liter next to the 10 fl oz volume. The title and the item volume say 10 fl oz. This guide uses that size and does not reconcile the extra capacity line. Nikwax's UK page for the Tech Wash and TX.Direct pair calls that two-bottle system PFAS-free. A different Amazon Tech Wash ASIN, B08DH52WTB, was unavailable when checked, and its bullet said "no PFCs" rather than PFAS-free. The 10 fl oz listing is the one whose bullet uses the class name.

The spray is a weaker match on the words. Nikwax's product index describes TX.Direct Spray-On as non-flammable, PFC-free, and aerosol-free. It does not, on that index line, say PFAS-free. The wash-in page does. Patagonia's care note says wash-in and spray-on re-proofers can both work if the spray actually covers the garment, and it says to use a re-proofing solution that does not use PFAS. The note names Storm Proof. This guide did not review a Storm Proof listing, so it does not link one.

Patagonia also says a machine dryer on low, about half an hour, reactivates a DWR, and that an iron on low, without steam, and with a cloth between the iron and the jacket, is the backup if there is no dryer. Nikwax says its wash-in develops water repellency on air drying. Follow the care label on the jacket and the instructions on the bottle you are using. Those two pages are not a single recipe.

Toxic-Free Future's waterproofing note says an old can of Scotchgard made by 3M likely contains PFAS, and it points to products Ecology identified as safer, with Nikwax as the example that can be washed in or sprayed on. Ecology's determination says it found one PFAS-free DWR among the safer alternatives for most apparel. The determination does not, in the passage this guide relies on, print a brand name. The Nikwax sentence is Toxic-Free Future's, tied to that Ecology work.

## What should you do with an old rain jacket?

Keep wearing it if it still works. The sale bans do not require you to throw out a jacket you already own.

California and New York both limit the prohibition to new, not previously used, articles. A used jacket is outside those sale rules. Washington's restriction, when it starts on January 1, 2027, is a rule for products sold in the state, not a pickup order for a hall closet. Nothing on the EPA or ATSDR pages this guide read says to discard a rain shell as household hazardous waste.

Washing is the pathway to take seriously if the jacket is the fluorinated kind the 2022 report describes. That report says treated apparel sheds PFAS into laundry water and that the chemicals are not fully removed at a wastewater plant. Re-treating with a PFAS-free wash-in refreshes the beading. It does not pull yesterday's fluorinated finish back out of the fiber. If the jacket still beads after a Tech Wash cycle, Nikwax says you can stop there and skip the extra treatment.

When the jacket is actually finished, the disposal fact these sources support is the landfill one. Toxic-Free Future says broken-down surface treatments in landfilled textiles are a long-term source of PFAS in leachate. Ecology lists disposal as one of the release points a restriction is meant to shrink. Using a shell you already have, and repairing the finish with a treatment that is not itself a new PFAS product, is the step that avoids both a new fluorinated jacket and a premature trip to the landfill. This guide did not find a take-back program it could verify for a PFAS-treated shell, so it does not name one.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do rain jackets contain PFAS?

Some do. Toxic-Free Future found PFAS in 9 of 13 jackets and 15 of 20 outdoor apparel items labeled stain- or water-resistant in its January 2022 tests. Two rain jackets in that set, from Mammut and The North Face, were below the fluorine screen. A current "made without intentionally added PFAS" line is a later claim about a named product, not a reversal of that test.

### What is the difference between DWR and the membrane?

DWR is the surface finish that makes water bead on the outside. The membrane is the layer that blocks rain from crossing the fabric while letting vapor out. Toxic-Free Future says a jacket can have a PFAS treatment, a PFAS membrane, or both. Patagonia says the face fabric can look wet while the membrane still keeps the inside dry.

### Is Gore-Tex free of PFAS?

Only when the product is the ePE version, and even then the claim has a limit. Gore's product page says the ePE membrane and its DWR are made without intentionally added PFAS and may contain trace amounts. Gore's March 27, 2025 statement says PTFE, used in many of its fabrics, is a PFAS, and that most consumer fabrics would use ePE by the end of 2025. A Gore-Tex label that does not say ePE has not made that claim. The 2022 Gore-Tex jackets tested at 4 to 8 percent fluorine.

### Does PFOA-free mean the jacket is PFAS-free?

No. PFOA is one chemical in the class. Patagonia says its move from C8 chemistry, including PFOA, to shorter-chain C6 was a regrettable substitution because C6 still contains fluorocarbons. PTFE can remain in a membrane after PFOA is gone. Gore calls PTFE a PFAS. Look for a PFAS or PTFE sentence, and for which layer it covers.

### Did the 2022 tests find PFAS in most waterproof jackets?

In that set, yes. Nine of 13 jackets contained PFAS, and total fluorine in the jacket group ran as high as 8 percent. The set was 11 rain jackets, one insulated jacket, and one wind jacket, bought mainly at REI and Dick's Sporting Goods. It was not a sample of every jacket on sale now. Across all stain- and water-resistant items in the study, 34 of 47 contained PFAS.

### Does PFAS in a rain jacket soak in through the skin?

ATSDR says dermal absorption is limited and does not appear to be a significant route for the general population. The routes it and EPA describe for consumer products are swallowing residue or dust, and the wider routes of water, food, and air. Toxic-Free Future and Washington Ecology describe release during manufacture, washing, and disposal. This guide has no measurement of a skin dose from one jacket.

### Does California ban PFAS in rain jackets in 2025?

It restricts new textile articles that contain regulated PFAS, beginning January 1, 2025. A general-use rain jacket is apparel, so it is in that rule. Regulated PFAS means intentionally added PFAS, or total organic fluorine at or above 100 ppm in 2025 and 50 ppm in 2027. Outdoor apparel for severe wet conditions, expert gear not marketed for general use, is exempt until January 1, 2028, and must say "Made with PFAS chemicals" if it still contains regulated PFAS.

### Is New York's rain-jacket rule the same as California's?

The dates match for the split. New York bans sale of new apparel with intentionally added PFAS after January 1, 2025, and the apparel definition includes ordinary outdoor apparel. Severe-wet expert outerwear is restricted on January 1, 2028. The DEC page reviewed for this guide does not show that a numeric limit has been adopted yet. California already has total-organic-fluorine thresholds. Both laws apply to new goods, not to a jacket you already own.

### What does Washington require for rain gear?

Beginning January 1, 2027, Ecology's rule restricts intentionally added PFAS in apparel and accessories sold in the state. Apparel for extreme and extended use is a reporting category, not part of that restriction. Ecology's 2024 determination said safer alternatives, including one PFAS-free DWR, were available for most apparel and were not shown for extended-use products.

### How do you re-waterproof a jacket without PFAS?

Wash it with a cleaner the maker calls PFAS-free. Nikwax says Tech Wash can revive a finish that is only dirty, and that TX.Direct Wash-In, which its page calls PFAS-free, is the next step in a separate cycle. The Amazon bullets for ASINs B000LN5O8O and B0019GOKK0 use the same PFAS-free wording. Skip an old Scotchgard can. Toxic-Free Future says one made by 3M likely contains PFAS.

### What should you do with an old waterproof jacket?

Keep using it. The California and New York bans cover new articles, not previously used ones. Washing a fluorinated shell can send PFAS out in the laundry water, which is a reason to avoid adding another fluorinated spray, not a reason the statute forces a disposal. When the jacket is worn out, landfill disposal is the long-term release Toxic-Free Future describes. This guide does not have a verified take-back program to name.

## Bottom line

A rain jacket contains PFAS when the DWR is a side-chain fluorinated polymer, when the membrane is PTFE, or when both are. The 2022 tests found that combination in most of the jackets they bought, and they found two name-brand rain jackets below the fluorine screen. Gore's current ePE claim and Patagonia's Spring 2025 claim are "made without intentionally added PFAS," with Gore's trace footnote still attached, and they are not a description of every older Gore-Tex or Torrentshell shell. PFOA-free leaves the rest of the class untouched. California and New York already restrict intentionally added PFAS in new general-use apparel, and both wait until 2028 for expert severe-wet outerwear. Washington's apparel restriction starts January 1, 2027. Skin contact is not the exposure route ATSDR treats as significant for the public. The practical steps these sources support are to read the finish and the membrane separately, buy a shell whose maker names both, and re-treat an old jacket with Tech Wash and TX.Direct Wash-In rather than a fluorinated spray.
