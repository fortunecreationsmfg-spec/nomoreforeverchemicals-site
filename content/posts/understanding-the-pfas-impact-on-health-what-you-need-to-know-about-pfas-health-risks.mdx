You’ve probably heard the term PFAS tossed around in the news or online. But what exactly are PFAS, and why should you care? These chemicals are everywhere, and they can affect your health in ways you might not expect. I want to break down the facts for you in a clear, simple way. By the end, you’ll understand the risks and know what steps you can take to protect yourself and your family.

## What Are PFAS and Why Are They Called Forever Chemicals?

PFAS stands for per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances. These are man-made chemicals used in many products because they resist heat, water, and oil. You’ll find PFAS in things like non-stick cookware, water-repellent clothing, food packaging, and even firefighting foam.

The problem? PFAS don’t break down easily. That’s why they’re called “forever chemicals.” They stick around in the environment and in your body for years. This means exposure can build up over time, increasing your risk of health problems.

## The Real PFAS Health Risks You Should Know

PFAS exposure has been linked to several health issues. Scientists are still studying these chemicals, but here’s what we know so far:

- **Immune system effects:** PFAS can weaken your immune response. This means your body might not fight infections as well.
- **Hormone disruption:** These chemicals can interfere with hormones that regulate growth, metabolism, and reproduction.
- **Increased cholesterol:** Studies show PFAS exposure can raise cholesterol levels, which is a risk factor for heart disease.
- **Cancer risk:** Some PFAS have been linked to kidney and testicular cancer.
- **Developmental problems:** Exposure during pregnancy may affect fetal growth and development.

You might wonder, “How do these chemicals get into my body?” The answer is through contaminated water, food, air, and even household products. It’s a hidden risk that many people don’t realize.

## How PFAS Enter Your Daily Life

You don’t have to work in a factory or live near a chemical plant to be exposed. PFAS are in everyday items and environments:

- **Drinking water:** Many water supplies have PFAS contamination. This is a major source of exposure.
- **Food packaging:** Fast food wrappers, microwave popcorn bags, and pizza boxes often contain PFAS to resist grease.
- **Cookware:** Non-stick pans coated with PFAS can release chemicals when heated.
- **Clothing and textiles:** Water-resistant jackets, carpets, and upholstery may contain PFAS.
- **Personal care products:** Some cosmetics and dental floss include PFAS for durability.

Knowing this helps you spot where PFAS might be lurking in your home.

## Practical Steps to Reduce Your PFAS Exposure

You might feel overwhelmed, but there are simple actions you can take to lower your risk:

1. **Check your water:** Get your tap water tested for PFAS. If contamination is found, consider using a water filter certified to remove PFAS.
2. **Choose safer cookware:** Switch to stainless steel, cast iron, or ceramic pans instead of non-stick.
3. **Read labels:** Avoid food packaging that mentions grease resistance or fluorinated chemicals.
4. **Buy PFAS-free products:** Look for clothing, furniture, and personal care items labeled PFAS-free.
5. **Stay informed:** Follow trusted sources like the [pfas blog](/blog) for updates and tips.

These steps can make a big difference in creating a safer, non-toxic home.
