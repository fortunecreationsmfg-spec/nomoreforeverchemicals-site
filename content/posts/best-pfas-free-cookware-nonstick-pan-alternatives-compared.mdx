**Quick answer:** If you want cookware with no PFAS coating, choose **cast iron, carbon steel, stainless steel, enameled cast iron, or ceramic-coated nonstick**. Cast iron and enameled cast iron are the best value for searing, roasting and braising. Stainless steel is the most versatile and lasts for decades. Ceramic nonstick is the easiest switch if you mainly cook eggs and delicate foods. Skip any pan that only says "PFOA-free," since that label tells you nothing about PTFE.

Below is a side-by-side comparison, what the labels actually mean, and the specific pans we link to in our [non-toxic products directory](/non-toxic-products).

## Is Teflon a PFAS?

Yes. Teflon is the brand name for polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), a fluoropolymer. The Minnesota Pollution Control Agency states plainly that PTFE is "one member of the PFAS family," and it falls inside the OECD's 2021 definition of PFAS, which covers any substance with a fully fluorinated carbon (–CF2– or –CF3).

So when you shop for PFAS-free cookware, "no PTFE" matters as much as "no PFAS."

## What does "PFOA-free" on a pan really mean?

Very little today. PFOA was a processing aid used to make PTFE coatings. Under the U.S. EPA's 2010/2015 PFOA Stewardship Program, the eight major manufacturers agreed to eliminate PFOA from emissions and product content by 2015, and all of them reported meeting that goal. A pan made since then is expected to be PFOA-free whether or not it says so, and it can still have a PTFE (PFAS) coating.

Labels that actually help:

- **"PTFE-free" or "PFAS-free"** on a coated pan.
- **Uncoated materials:** cast iron, carbon steel, stainless steel, enameled cast iron.
- **Ceramic (sol-gel) nonstick** from a brand that states the coating contains no PFAS.

## Is PTFE nonstick actually dangerous?

The honest answer has two parts.

**Normal use:** The FDA says nonstick coatings are polymerized and bonded to the pan at very high temperatures, and studies show only negligible amounts of PFAS can migrate from the coating into food.

**Overheating and damage:** Poison Control (National Capital Poison Center) notes that PTFE coatings start to break down above about 500°F, and that preheating an empty pan, cooking on high heat, broiling, and using scratched or chipped pans all raise the risk. Breathing those fumes can cause polymer fume fever ("Teflon flu"), with chills, fever, headache and body aches that usually clear in one to two days. US poison centers received a few hundred reports in 2023, and calls have been rising. The fumes can kill pet birds.

There's also the bigger picture. PFAS don't break down, and the fluoropolymer supply chain is a source of PFAS pollution. That is why some states now ban PFAS in cookware outright.

## Which states have banned PFAS cookware?

- **Minnesota:** Under Amara's Law, cookware with intentionally added PFAS can't be sold in the state as of January 1, 2025. That includes pots, pans, skillets, baking sheets and utensils.
- **Colorado:** Senate Bill 24-081 bans the sale of cookware with intentionally added PFAS as of January 1, 2026.
- **California:** A cookware ban (SB 682) passed the Legislature in 2025, but Governor Newsom vetoed it on October 13, 2025, citing the effect on affordable cookware, and the veto was sustained in March 2026.

## What is the best PFAS-free alternative to a nonstick frying pan?

For most kitchens, it's **a cast iron or carbon steel skillet** for everyday frying and **a ceramic nonstick pan** for eggs and fish. Cast iron's seasoning is just oil baked into a hard layer on the metal. It gets more nonstick the more you cook with it, works on every stovetop, and goes straight into the oven.

## Does stainless steel leach anything into food?

A little, mostly when it's new. A 2013 study in the *Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry* (Kamerud et al.) simmered tomato sauce in stainless steel and found that nickel and chromium leached into the sauce. Leaching was highest in new pans and dropped and leveled off after several cooking cycles. If you have a nickel allergy, consider cast iron, enameled cast iron, or nickel-free (18/0) stainless steel for long-simmered acidic dishes.

## What cookware should you avoid besides nonstick?

Avoid cheap, unlabeled aluminum or brass pots. The FDA has warned consumers not to use certain imported aluminum, brass and "Hindalium/Indalium" alloy cookware after testing showed it can leach lead into food, and it keeps a list of the affected products. The FDA says to throw those pieces away rather than donate them.

## How do you switch without replacing everything at once?

1. Replace any **scratched, flaking or warped nonstick pan** first. Those carry the most risk.
2. Add **one cast iron or carbon steel skillet** for daily frying and searing.
3. Add **one ceramic pan** for eggs if you still want easy release.
4. Move to **stainless or enameled cast iron** for pots as your old ones wear out.
5. Use **parchment** instead of nonstick bakeware.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ceramic cookware PFAS-free?

Ceramic (sol-gel) nonstick coatings are made without PTFE, and reputable brands state their coatings contain no PFAS. Check the specific product's listing or packaging for a "PFAS-free" or "PTFE-free" statement. A "PFOA-free" claim alone doesn't tell you.

### Is cast iron really nonstick?

Well-seasoned cast iron releases food well, especially once it's preheated and lightly oiled. It won't match brand-new PTFE for delicate eggs on day one, but it improves with use and never wears out the way a coating does.

### Is hard-anodized cookware PFAS-free?

Not necessarily. Hard-anodized aluminum is just the pan body, and many hard-anodized pans are coated with PTFE. Look for the coating type. For example, GreenPan's Valencia Pro uses a ceramic coating on a hard-anodized body.

### Should I throw out my nonstick pans right away?

The FDA authorizes PTFE coatings for food contact and says only negligible amounts migrate into food. The practical priority is to stop using pans that are scratched, flaking or warped, to never preheat an empty nonstick pan, and to keep high-heat cooking for cast iron or stainless steel. Replace the rest as they wear out.

### Do PFAS cookware bans affect pans I already own?

No. The Minnesota and Colorado laws restrict selling and distributing new cookware with intentionally added PFAS. They don't require you to get rid of pans you already have.

## Bottom line

You don't need a nonstick coating to cook well. A cast iron skillet, one stainless steel pot, an enameled Dutch oven and, if you want it, a ceramic pan for eggs will cover almost every meal without PTFE.
