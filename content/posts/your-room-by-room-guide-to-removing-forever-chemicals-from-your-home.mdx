## Kitchen — Cookware

Non-stick cookware is one of the most significant household sources of PFOA and PTFE. When scratched or overheated, PTFE-coated pans release fumes. PFOA was used in manufacturing and has been detected in blood serum in population studies.

### Swap 1: Non-stick frying pan → Cast Iron Skillet

PTFE begins to degrade above 500°F and releases fluorine-containing gases. Cast iron develops a natural polymerized oil layer that gives you non-stick performance without any fluoropolymer chemistry.

### Swap 2: Non-stick Dutch oven → Enameled Dutch Oven

PFOA (perfluorooctanoic acid) is associated with thyroid disruption and kidney effects at very low exposure levels. Enamel-coated cast iron uses no fluoropolymer chemistry at any stage of production.

### Swap 3: Non-stick griddle → PFAS-Free Griddle

Short-chain PFAS like PFBS (perfluorobutanesulfonic acid) — introduced as a "safer" replacement for PFOS — have been detected in cookware marketed as next-generation non-stick. Still under active EPA health assessment.

### Swap 4: Non-stick pot → Stainless Steel Pot

PFOS (perfluorooctanesulfonic acid) was used in stain-resistant coatings on some cookware. The EPA's 2024 drinking water rule set PFOS's maximum contaminant level at 4 parts per trillion. Stainless steel contains no fluoropolymer chemistry.

### Swap 5: Non-stick skillet set → Stainless Steel Skillet

GenX chemicals were developed as a PFOA replacement in fluoropolymer manufacturing and have been found in surface water near production facilities. Stainless steel removes fluoropolymer manufacturing from your cookware supply chain entirely.

## Water — Filtration

A 2023 USGS study estimated that at least 45% of U.S. tap water contains one or more PFAS compounds. These swaps target the filtration and storage steps where exposure is highest.

### Swap 1: Unfiltered tap → Under-Sink Reverse Osmosis Filter

PFOA and PFOS pass through standard carbon filters at rates as high as 90%. NSF/ANSI 58-certified reverse osmosis reduces PFOA and PFOS by over 90%. This is the highest-impact single water swap a household can make.

### Swap 2: Standard pitcher filter → Countertop Gravity Filter

PFNA (perfluorononanoic acid) and PFHxS (perfluorohexanesulfonic acid) — both regulated under the EPA's 2024 rule — are not consistently reduced by standard activated carbon pitcher filters. Gravity filters using activated alumina and carbon media show stronger results for this broader compound class.

### Swap 3: Unfiltered showerhead → Showerhead Filter

Inhalation and dermal absorption during showering are secondary PFAS exposure routes. Research in Environmental Science & Technology (2021) identified short-chain PFAS in shower aerosols. A KDF and activated carbon showerhead filter reduces chlorine by-products and some PFAS loading.

### Swap 4: Plastic water bottles → Stainless Steel or Glass Bottle

Some water-resistant coatings on food-contact plastics contain PFOA precursors. Stainless steel and borosilicate glass contain no fluoropolymer coatings and don't leach PFAS under any temperature condition.

### Swap 5: Unknown tap quality → Comprehensive PFAS Test Kit

PFOA, PFOS, PFNA, PFHxS, GenX, and PFBS — the six compounds now regulated under the EPA's 2024 rule — are odorless and tasteless. The only way to know your household's baseline is to test.

## Bathroom — Personal Care

A 2021 study in Environmental Science & Technology Letters found PFAS in more than half of U.S. cosmetics and personal care products tested. These swaps target the highest-use, highest-contact items.

### Swap 1: Conventional dental floss → PFAS-Free Dental Floss

PTFE coats many "smooth" flosses. A 2019 study in the Journal of Exposure Science & Environmental Epidemiology found women who used Oral-B Glide floss had measurably higher blood levels of PFHxS than non-users.

### Swap 2: Conventional deodorant → Natural Aluminum-Free Deodorant

PFAS including PFOA have been identified in personal care formulations as processing aids. Daily underarm application represents a consistent dermal exposure route.

### Swap 3: Chemical sunscreen → Mineral Sunscreen

PFAS compounds have been detected in water-resistant sunscreen formulations as coating agents. Mineral sunscreens (zinc oxide or titanium dioxide) achieve UV protection without synthetic polymer coatings.

### Swap 4: Conventional face cream → PFAS-Free Face Cream

PTFE and other PFAS are used in cosmetics as texture agents. The face has high skin permeability relative to other body surfaces. Choose a moisturizer formulated without fluoropolymer ingredients.

### Swap 5: Conventional mouthwash → Natural Alcohol-Free Mouthwash

Some commercial mouthwashes use synthetic preservatives in the same compound classes flagged in cosmetics PFAS screenings. Oral care products represent a daily mucosal exposure route.

## Cleaning

PFAS chemistry has been standard in stain-resistant and water-repellent treatments for decades. PFOS-based formulations were phased out after 2002, but their short-chain replacements are still in active use.

### Swap 1: Conventional all-purpose cleaner → Plant-Based All-Purpose Cleaner

PFAS-based surfactants including PFBS have been used in commercial cleaning formulations. PFBS was introduced as an alternative to PFOS but is now under EPA health assessment for kidney and developmental effects.

### Swap 2: Conventional laundry detergent → PFAS-Free Laundry Detergent

PFAS in fabric-care products are released into wastewater during washing; PFOA and PFOS have been detected downstream of laundry facilities. Switch to a detergent with no fluoropolymer surfactants.

### Swap 3: Dryer sheets → Wool Dryer Balls

Some dryer sheets use PFAS-derived antistatic compounds. Heat can volatilize these compounds, creating an inhalation exposure route. Wool dryer balls achieve softening and static reduction through mechanical action — no synthetic chemistry.

### Swap 4: Chlorine bleach → Oxygen Whitener

When bleach contacts PFAS-containing fabrics and surfaces, it can produce fluorinated transformation products. Sodium percarbonate (oxygen whitener) does not.

### Swap 5: Synthetic scrubbing pads → Natural Scrub Paste

PTFE fiber has been used in some scrubbing pads for non-scratch performance; when they shed, PTFE micro-particles enter the water system. A mineral and plant-based scrub paste achieves the same result without fluoropolymer fibers.

## Conclusion

Making these swaps can significantly reduce your exposure to PFAS. Start with one room at a time. Each small change contributes to a healthier home. Remember, knowledge is power. By understanding what products contain harmful chemicals, you can make informed decisions.
